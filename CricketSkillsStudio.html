How to bowl leg spin

A right-arm leg-spinner usually turns the ball from the leg side towards the off side for a right-handed batter. Left-arm wrist spin is the mirror-image variation. Grip and release matter more than trying to bowl fast.

🎯 Leg-spin action: step by step

  1. Grip: Rest the ball mainly between the index and middle fingers, with a relaxed grip. The ring finger supports and helps impart spin; the thumb rests lightly or stays relaxed.
  2. Approach: Use a comfortable, repeatable run-up. Keep your eyes on the target and build rhythm rather than rushing.
  3. Gather and alignment: Bring your bowling arm up naturally, coordinate the non-bowling arm, and keep your body balanced as you enter the delivery stride.
  4. Wrist position: Cock the wrist comfortably so the palm and fingers can work around the side of the ball. Avoid forcing the wrist into a painful position.
  5. Release: Rotate the forearm and wrist, allowing the ring finger to impart spin across the ball. Aim for a consistent release point and a looped flight.
  6. Follow-through: Let your bowling arm continue naturally and finish balanced, ready to field. Repeat the same action while changing line and length.
Coaching cue: first learn to land the ball on a good length with a relaxed, repeatable action. Turn and control are more useful than maximum effort.

Practice plan

Simple drills to build consistency

Use these as a starting point. Increase pace and difficulty only when your technique is controlled.

01 · Shadow batting

Practise stance, head position and a balanced finish for 3 sets of 10 repetitions without a ball.

02 · Straight-bat target

Use soft feeds or underarm throws to drive along the ground through a narrow target gate.

03 · Back-foot movement

Practise moving back and across before playing a controlled defensive shot or punch.

04 · Spin-bowling accuracy

From a short distance, land gentle leg-spin deliveries on a marked target before moving to full distance.

05 · Revolutions first

Practise spinning the ball from the fingers with a relaxed wrist. Focus on clean rotation, not speed.

06 · Line and length

Set safe target zones on a practice pitch. Record how many of 12 deliveries land in your chosen area.

Safety: practise in a clear space with suitable protective equipment and a coach or experienced partner where possible. Use a soft ball for early drills. Do not attempt high-risk shots or force wrist/shoulder movements through pain.
