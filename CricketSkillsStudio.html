Bowling masterclass

How to bowl leg spin

A right-arm leg-spinner usually turns the ball from the leg side towards the off side for a right-handed batter. Left-arm wrist spin is the mirror-image variation. Grip and release matter more than trying to bowl fast.

🎯 Leg-spin action: step by step

  1. Grip: Rest the ball mainly between the index and middle fingers, with a relaxed grip. The ring finger supports and helps impart spin; the thumb rests lightly or stays relaxed.
  2. Approach: Use a comfortable, repeatable run-up. Keep your eyes on the target and build rhythm rather than rushing.
  3. Gather and alignment: Bring your bowling arm up naturally, coordinate the non-bowling arm, and keep your body balanced as you enter the delivery stride.
  4. Wrist position: Cock the wrist comfortably so the palm and fingers can work around the side of the ball. Avoid forcing the wrist into a painful position.
  5. Release: Rotate the forearm and wrist, allowing the ring finger to impart spin across the ball. Aim for a consistent release point and a looped flight.
  6. Follow-through: Let your bowling arm continue naturally and finish balanced, ready to field. Repeat the same action while changing line and length.
Coaching cue: first learn to land the ball on a good length with a relaxed, repeatable action. Turn and control are more useful than maximum effort.

Batting foundations

Right-handed and left-handed batting

The core principles are the same for both: balance, watching the ball, judging length, moving the feet and keeping the head steady. The stance and off/leg sides are mirrored. Use the hand that gives you natural control; do not force a change without coaching.

🏏 Right-handed batter

  • Normally the left hand is the top hand on the handle and the right hand the bottom hand.
  • For a right-handed batter, the off side is to the batter's right when facing the bowler; the leg side is to the left.
  • Build a balanced stance, relaxed grip, still head and a clear view of the bowler's release.
  • Practise straight-bat defence, forward defence, back-foot defence and straight drive before advanced shots.

🏏 Left-handed batter

  • Normally the right hand is the top hand on the handle and the left hand the bottom hand.
  • The off side and leg side are mirrored compared with a right-handed batter.
  • Practise the same fundamentals with a mirrored stance; check grip and alignment with a coach.
  • Learn to score on both sides and communicate clearly with a right-handed partner when running.

For both handednesses, the safest and most effective technique is individual: stance width, trigger movement and grip comfort can vary. Focus on repeatable balance rather than copying a professional's exact style.
Pace bowling academy

Fast bowling, swing and seam

Pace bowling combines a repeatable run-up, legal action, stable alignment, wrist position, seam presentation and a balanced follow-through. Swing depends on the ball, seam, conditions and release; it cannot be guaranteed by one grip alone.

Fast-bowling action checklist

  • Use a run-up you can repeat without sprinting out of control.
  • Build a smooth gather and coordinated non-bowling-arm movement.
  • Keep the head and trunk stable through the delivery stride.
  • Release with a firm, comfortable wrist and a seam position suited to the delivery.
  • Follow through naturally in line with the target and finish balanced.
  • Develop speed gradually with strength, mobility and recovery work.
  • Stop and seek qualified advice for persistent back, shoulder, elbow or ankle pain.
  • Young and developing bowlers should follow age-appropriate workload guidance and coaching.

Spin bowling academy

Off spin and other spin skills

Spin bowling is about revolutions, flight, drift, dip, changes of pace and landing the ball in a challenging area. Build a reliable stock ball before adding variations.

🌀 Right-arm off spin: fundamentals

  1. Use a relaxed finger-spin grip; the index and middle fingers work across the seam, with the thumb resting lightly as comfortable.
  2. Keep the run-up and delivery stride balanced and repeatable.
  3. Use the fingers to impart revolutions; avoid squeezing the ball or forcing the wrist.
  4. Release with a consistent action and aim for a good length outside the batter's scoring zone.
  5. Develop flight and control first, then practise changes of pace and variations under coaching.
Watch off-spin tutorials ↗

🌀 Spin-bowling plan

  • Choose a stock ball and a clear target before each over.
  • Change one variable at a time: line, length, pace or flight.
  • Use a field that supports your plan and communicate with the captain.
  • Keep notes on which deliveries produce false shots, not only wickets.
  • Practise variations without telegraphing them through a large action change.

Fielding and wicketkeeping

Catch cleanly, move quickly, save runs

Good fielding is anticipation plus safe body position, soft hands, communication and repeatable footwork. Practise catching progressions with a suitable ball before increasing pace or difficulty.

🧤 How to take catches

  1. Get ready: Start balanced on the balls of the feet with hands relaxed and eyes on the batter or ball.
  2. Move early: Take small adjustment steps and get your body behind the ball when possible.
  3. Watch it in: Track the ball all the way into your hands; avoid closing your eyes or turning away.
  4. Make a soft pocket: For a high catch, fingers generally point up; for a low catch, fingers generally point down. Adapt hand position to the ball's path.
  5. Give with the ball: Relax the hands and elbows to absorb impact. Secure the ball before celebrating or throwing.
  6. Communicate: Call clearly, especially when two fielders converge, and avoid colliding.

🧤 Wicketkeeping essentials

  • Adopt a comfortable crouch and stay balanced, with eyes level.
  • Move the feet to get in line; do not reach with hands alone.
  • Give with the hands to receive the ball softly and bring it into the body.
  • For takes standing back, track bounce and carry; for standing up, train safely with a coach.
  • Practise takes, footwork, stumpings, run-outs and clear communication with bowlers.

Game intelligence

Match strategy, pressure and decision-making

A strong player does not try to win every ball. They choose a plan, communicate, notice patterns and reset after mistakes. Match tactics depend on format, conditions, rules, score and available players.

🧠 A simple pressure routine

  1. Pause: Take one comfortable breath and relax your shoulders.
  2. Focus: Pick one controllable cue—watch the release, move early, or hit the target.
  3. Plan: Decide the next ball's intention, not the whole match's outcome.
  4. Commit: Trust the plan and avoid changing it at the last instant without new information.
  5. Reset: After the ball, review one thing briefly and return attention to the next delivery.

If nerves rise, that is normal. Use a repeatable routine, talk to your captain or coach, and focus on effort and decisions rather than only the scoreboard.

📋 Match-day checklist

  • Know the format, target, wickets remaining, overs and required rate.
  • Agree batting partnerships, calling words and running risks before play.
  • For batters: identify scoring zones and a safe option for a good ball.
  • For bowlers: set a plan for each batter and review field placement with the captain.
  • For fielders: know the boundary, backing-up role and likely batter tendencies.
  • After play: note two strengths and one practical improvement.
Preparation and recovery

Fitness, warm-up, nutrition and injury prevention

Cricket needs repeated short bursts, throwing capacity, mobility, concentration and recovery. Training should match your age, experience and health status; use a qualified coach or health professional when needed.

🔥 Warm-up

🏃

Prepare gradually before batting, bowling or fielding.

  • Begin with light movement to raise body temperature.
  • Add dynamic mobility for ankles, hips, thoracic spine and shoulders.
  • Progress into short accelerations, easy throws and sport-specific movements.

💪 Strength and mobility

🏋️

Build a durable body with balanced, progressive training.

  • Include legs, trunk, upper back and shoulder-control exercises.
  • Use sound technique and sensible loads.
  • Fast bowlers need particular attention to workload and recovery.

💧 Fuel and recover

🥗

Support long sessions with everyday basics.

  • Drink regularly and adjust for heat and sweat.
  • Eat familiar meals and snacks that provide energy and protein.
  • Prioritise sleep, rest days and gradual return after injury.
This is general sports education, not medical advice. Do not bowl through pain or sudden loss of function. Seek qualified assessment for persistent pain, dizziness, concussion symptoms or significant injury.
Understand the match

Formats, roles and cricket vocabulary

The best approach changes with the match format and situation. Learn the language used by players, coaches and umpires.

🏟️ Test cricket

Patience, leaving well, bowling long spells, creating pressure and protecting energy over multiple days. A draw can be a valid result.

⏱️ One-day cricket

Balance wickets and run rate, build partnerships, rotate strike, use fielding restrictions strategically and plan bowling phases.

⚡ T20 cricket

Know match-ups, boundary options, risk-reward choices, death-over plans and the value of wickets in hand.

📣 Communication

Call “yes”, “no” or “wait” loudly and early when running. Back up, watch your partner and avoid both batters stopping in the same area.

🎯 Bowling terms

Line is the direction of the delivery; length is where it pitches; swing is movement in the air; seam movement occurs after pitching; spin changes rotation and bounce.

📏 Fielding positions

Learn slips, gully, point, cover, mid-off, mid-on, midwicket, square leg, fine leg, third man, long-off and long-on. Positioning depends on the plan and rules.

Practice plan

Simple drills to build consistency

Use these as a starting point. Increase pace and difficulty only when your technique is controlled.

01 · Shadow batting

Practise stance, head position and a balanced finish for 3 sets of 10 repetitions without a ball.

02 · Straight-bat target

Use soft feeds or underarm throws to drive along the ground through a narrow target gate.

03 · Back-foot movement

Practise moving back and across before playing a controlled defensive shot or punch.

04 · Spin-bowling accuracy

From a short distance, land gentle leg-spin deliveries on a marked target before moving to full distance.

05 · Revolutions first

Practise spinning the ball from the fingers with a relaxed wrist. Focus on clean rotation, not speed.

06 · Line and length

Set safe target zones on a practice pitch. Record how many of 12 deliveries land in your chosen area.

07 · Catching progression

Begin with gentle underarm feeds, then practise high catches and low catches with a suitable ball.

08 · Throwing to a target

Practise a balanced pickup, alignment and accurate throw at increasing distances. Warm up the shoulder first.

09 · Match scenarios

Play short scenarios: defend 8 runs in the final over, or chase a target with wickets in hand. Discuss options after each ball.

10 · Pressure routine

Before each practice ball, breathe, choose one cue, execute, then reset. Keep the routine short and repeatable.

Safety: practise in a clear space with suitable protective equipment and a coach or experienced partner where possible. Use a soft ball for early drills. Do not attempt high-risk shots or force wrist/shoulder movements through pain.
